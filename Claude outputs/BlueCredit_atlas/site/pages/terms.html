2. Cancellations & refunds

How to cancel

  • In your BlueCredit account, open Plans & billing and choose Cancel subscription (from Atlas: click your name at the top right, then Plans & billing); or
  • In PayPal, go to Settings → Payments → Manage automatic payments and cancel "BlueCredit".

Cancelling stops all future payments. You keep Pro access until the end of the period you already paid for, and then your account returns to a free account. Your free account and sign-in stay active.

Refunds

  • Payments already made are generally non-refundable, and we don't give partial refunds for unused time.
  • Charged by mistake? If you were charged in error, charged after cancelling, or subscribed by accident, email us within 14 days of the charge and we will refund it.
  • Yearly subscribers who cancel within 14 days of a new yearly charge can ask for a full refund of that charge.
  • Refunds go back to the original payment method through PayPal and usually appear within 5–10 business days.

Estimates and projections are modeled and may differ from actual water use.

7. Data sources

Every dataset and live service used on the map and in reports.

Water demand

Domestic, industrial, agricultural and power plant water use: Dieter, C.A., Linsey, K.S., Caldwell, R.R., Harris, M.A., Ivahnenko, T.I., Lovelace, J.K., Maupin, M.A., and Barber, N.L. (2018). Estimated Use of Water in the United States County-Level Data for 2015 (ver. 2.0, June 2018). U.S. Geological Survey data release. https://doi.org/10.5066/F7TB15V5. County withdrawals in million gallons per day. Industrial includes mining; agricultural is irrigation, livestock and aquaculture; domestic is self-supplied plus public-supply deliveries to homes. County boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau via us-atlas.

Industrial facilities: U.S. EPA, Toxics Release Inventory (TRI) Basic Data File, 2024 (reporting processed through November 2025): facility location, industry sector and pounds of reportable chemicals released to water, transferred to sewage plants and released in total. Sectors grouped by BlueCredit. epa.gov/tri

Power plant cooling water: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Thermoelectric cooling water data, 2024 (Forms EIA-860 and EIA-923; released November 2025): annual water withdrawal and consumption by plant; plant locations from EIA's Power Plants in the U.S. eia.gov/electricity/data/water

Higher education (universities & colleges): U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data (HIFLD), Colleges and Universities, from the National Center for Education Statistics IPEDS (enrollment, dorm capacity, employees). Public domain.

Hospitality: Lodging Econometrics hotel census and development pipeline (licensed data; contact details removed). Shown to Pro users only.

Facility water estimates: planning-level only. Hotels: 33,500 gallons per guest room per year (median); schools: 11,200 gallons per worker per year (median), from WaterSense and ENERGY STAR, U.S. Water Use Intensity by Property Type: Technical Reference (June 2023). Dorms: 82 gallons per person per day at home (EPA WaterSense, from USGS 2015) for a 270-day academic year.

Data center datasets

Mongird, K., Vernon, C., Burleyson, C., Akdemir, K. Z., & Rice, J. (2026). IM3 Open Source Data Center Atlas (v2026.02.09) [Data set]. MSD-LIVE Data Repository. https://doi.org/10.57931/3017294. Derived from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0.

Mongird, K., Burleyson, C., Akdemir, K. Z., Vernon, C., & Rice, J. (2026). IM3 Projected US Data Center Locations (v1.1) [Data set]. MSD-LIVE Data Repository. https://doi.org/10.57931/3020186. CC BY 4.0.

Live map services

Watersheds (HUC-8): U.S. Geological Survey, Watershed Boundary Dataset (WBD), The National Map. hydro.nationalmap.gov

Rivers & streams: U.S. Geological Survey, National Hydrography Dataset (NHD), The National Map. hydro.nationalmap.gov

Wetlands: U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, National Wetlands Inventory (NWI). fws.gov/program/national-wetlands-inventory

Water utility service areas: U.S. EPA, Community Water Service Area Boundaries (Q2 2025 SDWIS). © 2025 US EPA, MIT License.

Recycled water lines: Loudoun Water GIS Department, Reclaimed Water Pipe Line.

Sewersheds: U.S. EPA, National Sewershed Dataset (Clean Watersheds Needs Survey program): boundaries of the area draining to each publicly owned treatment works, either provided by utilities and states or modeled by EPA. Public domain (MIT license). epa.gov/cwns/sewersheds

Flood zones: FEMA, National Flood Hazard Layer (effective Flood Insurance Rate Map data), public domain. Not a substitute for the official FIRM or a FIRMette for insurance or permits. msc.fema.gov

Flood risk: FEMA, National Risk Index (inland and coastal flooding risk ratings, scores and expected annual loss by county and census tract), public domain. hazards.fema.gov/nri. Flood insurance claims: OpenFEMA, NFIP Redacted Claims v3. fema.gov/openfema

Dams & reservoirs: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, National Inventory of Dams (NGDA), via Esri U.S. Federal Data (checked weekly), CC BY 4.0. Purpose, storage, hazard potential, condition assessment and inspection dates as reported by state and federal dam safety agencies. nid.sec.usace.army.mil

Wastewater treatment plants: U.S. EPA, 2022 Clean Watersheds Needs Survey (CWNS), national data release (Sept 2026). Design capacity, population served, treatment level, discharge/reuse and documented needs as reported to CWNS. epa.gov/cwns

Drought: U.S. Drought Monitor, jointly produced by the National Drought Mitigation Center at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, the U.S. Department of Agriculture and NOAA. Current weekly map and weekly history since 2000 (via Esri Living Atlas). droughtmonitor.unl.edu

Streamflow vs. normal: NOAA / NWS Office of Water Prediction, National Water Model analysis and assimilation (experimental NWC Visualization Service "ana_anomaly", 7-day layer). Each stream reach is coloured by its 7-day average flow as a percentile of modeled flows for the same calendar day; reaches between the 26th and 75th percentile (normal) are not shaded. Model estimates, updated daily; not USGS gauge measurements. Public domain. water.noaa.gov

Streamgages: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Data for the Nation — live streamflow (parameter 00060) and gage height (00065) at continuously monitored stations, plus 10 years of daily mean flow, via the USGS Water Data OGC APIs. Status compares the latest flow with daily means for the same time of year (±7 days). Provisional data. waterdata.usgs.gov

Groundwater: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Data for the Nation — daily depth to water at continuously monitored wells (parameter 72019), via the USGS Water Data OGC APIs. Status compares the latest reading with the same time of year (±15 days) in the well's last 10 years. waterdata.usgs.gov

Reclaimed water end uses and providers: U.S. Department of Energy, Federal Energy Management Program (FEMP), Reclaimed Wastewater Providers (utility data 2009–2012, AZ, CA, CO, FL, NV, TX; published June 30, 2016). Matched to CWNS plants within ~2 miles; unmatched providers shown with approximate locations. energy.gov/femp

Satellite imagery: © Google.

Street map: Esri World Street Map — Esri, HERE, Garmin, USGS, Intermap, INCREMENT P, NRCan, Esri Japan, METI, Esri China (Hong Kong), Esri Korea, Esri (Thailand), NGCC, © OpenStreetMap contributors, and the GIS User Community.

Address search: Photon (komoot) and Nominatim geocoders, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

Map software: Leaflet · Esri Leaflet (Powered by Esri) · Turf.js · leaflet-control-geocoder.

Notes

IM3 is led by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory with support from the U.S. DOE Office of Science (MultiSector Dynamics). Projected sites are model outputs (CERF – Data Centers), not announced facilities, and are not for site-specific planning. Watershed, water utility, wetland and river/lake links for IM3 sites are looked up live from the USGS, EPA and USFWS services listed above when a popup opens; distances are straight-line from the facility centroid. Data center water-use volumes, restoration projects and the Loudoun Water aggregates come from public-records (FOIA) responses and utility data compiled by BlueCredit and are not part of the datasets above. County water demand by sector is the USGS 2015 estimate, the most recent release covering every sector; newer USGS model estimates cover only some sectors.
